The idea

Let the network learn its own wiring

A standard network connects every neuron to every neuron of the next layer. Many targets are built from small parts that each use only a few inputs. LeWi lets a network find that structure by learning which neurons connect.

  1. 1Place

    Every neuron gets a position in a small 3-D space, learned like any other parameter.

  2. 2Connect

    Neurons in adjacent layers connect only when they are closer than a fixed radius. Closer pairs get a stronger gate.

  3. 3Rewire

    Training moves the positions with the weights. Neurons that should work together move closer and the others move apart, so the wiring changes as the network learns.

The result is a network whose wiring follows the structure of its target, learned from input–output examples alone, and which needs fewer samples on compositional targets than a dense network.

Method

Connectivity from neuron positions

Each neuron i in layer ℓ has a learned embedding ei(ℓ)​∈Rr. Neurons in adjacent layers connect only when their embeddings lie within a fixed radius R. The prediction loss trains the embeddings and the weights together, so training itself decides which connections exist.

  1. Connection score

    sij(ℓ)​=R2−​ei(ℓ+1)​−ej(ℓ)​​22​

    Positive inside the radius, zero or negative beyond it.

  2. Weight used in the forward pass

    Wij(ℓ)​=Wij(ℓ)​σ(sij(ℓ)​)1{sij(ℓ)​>0}

    A soft sigmoid gate scales each active weight; a binary mask switches the weight off at the radius.

  3. How the loss moves the neurons

    −∇ei​​L=2j∈N(i)∑​∣Wij​∣σ′(sij​)∂∣Wij​∣∂L​(ei​−ej​)

    Every active connection pulls its two neurons together when a stronger connection would lower the loss, and pushes them apart when a weaker one would. Because one embedding takes part in many connections, a single update coordinates many wiring decisions. A connection beyond the radius gets no direct gradient; it can return when other connections move its neurons back within R. Masks are recomputed after every step.

Defaults r=3 · e∼N(0,σe2​I), σe​=0.5 · R2=2rσe2​=1.5, fixed during training · the same rule on every layer transition, the readout included.

Benchmark

CSFRBench: hidden structure, kept for diagnosis

Compositionally sparse function regression: each target is a composition of low-arity local functions on a directed acyclic graph. The learner sees only input–output examples. The graph and every intermediate value are kept, so one benchmark measures both sample efficiency and whether a network’s internal organization matches the target computation.

inputlocal functionreadout

  • Six structural regimes. Five fixed graph templates and a distribution of sampled depth-4 DAGs, from parallel low-arity modules to hierarchy, reuse and a high-arity control.
  • Graph-informed references. GraphOracle(ℓ) networks use the same backbone with fixed masks from the first ℓ levels of the true graph, which shows how much target connectivity is worth.
  • One protocol for every method. 32 scrambled-Sobol hyperparameter trials per method, target and sample size; the checkpoint and trial with the lowest validation NMSE are evaluated once on a 65,536-example test set.

Results · sample efficiency

Fewer samples on compositional targets

Relative to dense networks, LeWi lowers the mean area under the learning curve by 20% on the depth-4 tree, 34% on the reuse DAG, 32% on the depth-5 tree and 27% across 32 sampled DAGs. The gain is smaller on flat modules, and dense networks are better on the single high-arity node, which has no low-arity structure to exploit.

On the depth-4 tree LeWi closes 51% of the gap between Dense and GraphOracle(all), against 26% for L0​. ▲ marks a mean above the plotted range (Dense on the depth-5 tree at n=211: 4.2).

Results · learned structure

The learned wiring mirrors the target computation

Measured against the target graph

We extract a module graph from the learned embeddings and masks alone, by clustering neurons at the connection radius, and compare it with the target graph. Trained LeWi reaches more than three times the edge F1 of its initialization and of randomized controls on average across four structured settings. For edges that start at target intermediates, which input selection alone cannot explain, mean F1 is 0.473.

The hidden layers carry the target computation too: target intermediates are more linearly decodable from LeWi than from dense networks, and counterfactual interventions on target-aligned subspaces (Boundless DAS) agree more closely with the target computation.

Results · beyond the benchmark

The gain carries over to world-model prediction

On 17 state-based prediction tasks from the stable-worldmodel suite, each model predicts the change of state from the current state and action and is rolled out for 16 steps. LeWi improves over the dense baseline on 16 tasks and wins 99 of 119 task–baseline comparisons. Gains are strongest on low-dimensional control and navigation; most ties and losses occur on articulated control with many coupled joints and on manipulation.
